# Proposed Experimentation Maturity Assessment

## Purpose

This is a diagnostic assessment, not a quiz. It is designed to show how a company currently operates across five dimensions of digital experimentation:

1. Scale and reach
2. Strategy and value
3. Process and decision-making
4. Culture, leadership, and capability
5. Data, methods, and technology

The questions synthesize the resources in this directory into observable, reasonably easy-to-answer behaviors. They intentionally assess maturity across several dimensions rather than treating test volume or statistical sophistication as maturity on its own.

## Suggested respondent guidance

- Answer for the part of the company you know best (for example, a product line, business unit, or region).
- Unless a question says otherwise, choose the answer that best reflects the **last three months**, not a future plan or isolated best case.
- If practices vary by team, choose the answer that describes the **typical** team.
- “Experiment” means a controlled test intended to estimate causal impact. Feature rollouts without a control group do not count.
- “Not sure” should always be available and should not be scored.

## Product and scoring notes

- Keep the answer choices in behavioral language. Do not label them “beginner” through “advanced” in the respondent experience.
- Score by dimension as well as overall. A company may have strong technology but weak culture, or high volume but weak rigor; the result should expose those constraints.
- Use the option order as an initial ordinal scale (A=1 through E=5), but calibrate thresholds with real response data before publishing benchmarks.
- Do not score “Not sure” as the lowest answer. Treat it as missing data and surface low-confidence dimensions separately.
- Consider asking respondents to select their scope, role, company size, digital audience size, and industry before the assessment. Use these for segmentation, not maturity points.
- Avoid making raw test count a dominant factor. Questions 2-4 distinguish scale, breadth, and default behavior from company size.
- In the results, recommend the next one or two operating constraints to address rather than presenting a single judgmental grade.
